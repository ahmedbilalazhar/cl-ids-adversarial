\hypertarget{abstract}{%
\section{Abstract}\label{abstract}}

We implement a five-client federated class-incremental intrusion
detector and investigate training-label corruption during acquisition of
a new attack family. The main development-bank study contains 696 GPU
trajectories across three ownership partitions and four training
replicates per partition. A further 12 full-budget CPU trajectories
provide a separate completed evaluation of post-attack replay-memory
interventions. Additional controls are included below only when
completed and validated. DER++-style replay attains clean final eligible
macro-F1 0.491, while fine-tuning and the EWC-style variant largely
forget earlier classes. The attack produces targeted errors after
withdrawal; class protection preserves admission counts without
guaranteeing effective acquisition. The CPU supplement separates
continuing poisoned replay from the common poisoned stage-E2 model using
oracle versus count-matched random deletion. Findings are conditional on
one corrected CICIDS2017 split; no final-test or independent-site
generalization is claimed.

\hypertarget{research-questions-and-contribution}{%
\section{1. Research questions and
contribution}\label{research-questions-and-contribution}}

An intrusion detector must learn newly annotated attack families while
retaining older ones. Federated learning adds heterogeneous ownership,
and replay can retain both useful examples and corrupted labels. RQ1
asks how learning mechanisms differ in immediate source-family damage
and persistence after attacker withdrawal. RQ2 asks whether class-aware
admission improves the trade-off between targeted error and legitimate
acquisition. RQ3 asks which replay and selection mechanisms explain
these effects. The contribution is a controlled empirical study with
explicit failure cases, rather than a claim of a generally successful
defense or exhaustive novelty.

\hypertarget{dataset-preprocessing-and-isolation}{%
\section{2. Dataset, preprocessing and
isolation}\label{dataset-preprocessing-and-isolation}}

The source is the authors' corrected CICIDS2017 release, pinned by
SHA-256. Attempted-category remapping is applied under the adopted
annotation policy before conflict handling. Invalid/nonfinite features
are excluded under the named preparation policy; forward and backward
header-length totals are omitted. A Monday-benign-only min/max transform
is frozen before later stages, with zero-range denominator one and no
clipping or later refit. This is an ordered annotated-class benchmark,
not evidence of live zero-day discovery.

Sessions linked within the declared 120-second interval and
exact-content links form indivisible connected components. The fixed
grouped five-fold split assigns folds 2/3/4 to training, fold 1 to
development, and fold 0 to final test. Persistent whole-component
ownership assigns five simulated clients using family-skew targets.
Components do not cross the declared roles; this does not demonstrate
host, campaign, or site independence. No final-test path is read by the
supplement. Fresh checks find 183,045 unique training IDs per partition,
272,506 development rows, and zero training/development ID
intersections. These ID checks supplement, rather than replace, the
prior content/component preparation audit.

\hypertarget{implemented-methodology}{%
\section{3. Implemented methodology}\label{implemented-methodology}}

The shared classifier is an MLP with 74 inputs, hidden widths 128 and
64, ReLU and dropout 0.1. Output slots are append-only and expand 3
-\textgreater{} 8 -\textgreater{} 13 -\textgreater{} 16. Raw source
codes and output-slot indices are distinct. Five clients train locally
and the server performs current-admitted-row-weighted FedAvg. Ownership
persists across stages. At every broadcast Adam is reset, so optimizer
state is not silently carried between server rounds.

\hypertarget{threat-and-admission-controls}{%
\subsubsection{3.1 Threat and admission
controls}\label{threat-and-admission-controls}}

In E2, seeded nested subsets of DoS Hulk records on selected clients are
relabelled benign. Training and admission see supplied labels only.
E3/E4 receive no new corruption; historical poison may survive. Attack
arms vary both client count and within-client dose, and global flipped
fractions depend on ownership. A one-client arm cannot be interpreted as
interchangeable with an equal-size five-client corruption. The threat
assumes compliant training agents; it does not cover arbitrary forged
updates, server compromise, or an adaptive filter-aware adversary.

At each stage, the frozen expanded model scores admission before
training. DC admits all new-labelled and small classes and retains the
lowest-loss rows within other per-class quotas (default fraction 0.75,
small-class threshold 20). DM uses global loss ranking with exactly the
same per-client volume and aggregation weight. DQ uses DC's class quotas
but selects randomly within class. NONE admits all rows. Poisoned Hulk
rows bear supplied benign labels and therefore do not receive new-class
protection. Retaining all honestly new-labelled rows is a construction
check, not proof of learning.

The independent confusion-matrix audit verifies archived accuracy,

Acquisition is recomputed on the same corrected population.

Every attack outcome uses the clean twin with identical learner,
admission, component ownership, replicate and variant. GPU and CPU
comparisons are kept separate. Within a fixed ownership partition, the
replicate index changes model, minibatch, poisoning and replay random
streams; it is not solely optimizer noise. For paired sensitivity
analyses, these replicates are averaged within each ownership partition.
Three partition-level effects are shown directly; conditional
signed-rank sign enumeration handles zeros and average-rank ties
explicitly. It assumes symmetric differences and exchangeable signs and
does not create independent captures. All-zero effects have p=1 and
undefined rank-biserial effect. Combined Holm correction covers 47
amended GPU contrasts, with a separate declared family of 15 CPU
mechanism contrasts. With only three partition units, two-sided exact
sign enumeration cannot produce p\textless0.05. Nonsignificance is not
equivalence, immunity, or evidence of no mechanism. No narrow bootstrap
confidence claim is made from three partitions.

\hypertarget{new-cpu-mechanism-and-learnability-experiments}{%
\section{7. New CPU mechanism and learnability
experiments}\label{new-cpu-mechanism-and-learnability-experiments}}

The supplement completed 12 full-budget CPU trajectories. The main
four-condition mechanism experiment uses three ownership partitions x 1
training replicate(s). DQ and offline pooled checks are reported only
when all their planned runs have completed; they are otherwise pending
and excluded from numerical claims. All comparisons within the
supplement use CPU clean references and the same current source/input
identity. The runtime and source manifests identify exact software
versions, files, configurations and SHA-256 values; each completed
result is written atomically and validated before reuse.

At five attackers and 50\% E2 corruption, ordinary replay, oracle
deletion and count-matched random deletion begin from identical end-E2
model parameters, buffer tensors, labels, logits, poison flags and RNG
states. State fingerprints and E1/E2 metrics must match exactly or the
run is rejected. The oracle deletes evaluator-marked poisoned records;
random deletion removes the same number per client using an
intervention-only RNG. Both compact all buffer fields, preserve
historical proposal counts and refill vacancies on subsequent
insertions. This is a memory-content intervention with declared sampling
consequences, not standard reservoir sampling of the entire original
history after deletion. No new poison is introduced during E3/E4.

For the most direct mechanism contrast, oracle-minus-random H

composition changes, or establish a deployable purification method.
Random deletion matches volume but not retained class composition, so
the contrast identifies the targeted removal policy rather than a pure
label-only effect. Null or mixed effects remain informative limits.

Across the completed mechanism grid, mean targeted persistence is 0.000
after oracle purge versus 0.474 after matched random deletion.
Oracle-minus-random H is negative in 3 of three partition means. Compare
final macro-F1 as well: 0.468 for oracle versus 0.377 for random. The
result is interpreted within the fixed continuation and the
oracle-access boundary, rather than as a general poisoning-removal
guarantee.

Communication is an analytical float32 parameter-exchange count,
excluding transport overhead, not measured packet traffic or network
latency. GPU times come from concurrent workers; CPU times come from
sequential single-thread runs under a different runtime. Neither
supports a hardware speedup claim. Replay bytes count stored numeric
tensors, not total Python/process memory or peak RSS. Real edge latency
and energy were not measured.

The evidence supports a scoped retention/security trade-off: mechanisms
that preserve older behavior can also retain attack-associated errors.
Admission protection alone does not guarantee useful discrimination, and
low additional damage may reflect already-lost capability.
Oracle-versus-random deletion improves mechanism identification relative
to memory-size correlation, but its interpretation remains conditional
on changed buffer contents and the tested post-withdrawal stream.

Threats include a single historical capture, fixed development bank,
three simulated ownership partitions, one source/target label pattern,
outcome-informed expansion of attack severity, and development-only
evaluation. Shared captures and repeated training do not establish
independent deployments. Rare annotations have limited or zero
evaluation support. No adaptive adversary, forged-update defense,

discovery or state-of-the-art superiority is claimed. The final-test
split was not opened for these experiments.
